# Common School Archive: research and mapping report

**Research snapshot: November 17, 2026.** This report describes a bounded investigation of New York common-school records, especially the relationship between local district decisions and state records of district organization. It is a working research report, not a complete transcription, statewide inventory, legal boundary dataset, or account of every schoolhouse. Sources and claims have different levels of certainty; the distinctions below are part of the findings.

## Executive summary

- The principal source examined is an accessible [116-position excerpt of State Archives series B0494](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/zmuhls/cs-archive/main/raw/scans/NYS%20Archives/B0494/District-Consolidation-Data_100-116.pdf), a register of school-district establishment, consolidation, and centralization. It contains two front-matter positions and 114 register images filed under 29 counties, Broome through Onondaga. Six visually matched retake pairs leave 108 apparent distinct ledger surfaces. Surfaces, rows, and actions are different units; the excerpt is not the whole physical volume.
- Nineteen selected cases or grouped entries from 13 filing counties were checked against the scans. They demonstrate distinct procedural stages, cross-county districts, different institutional forms, and some explicitly reversed orders. They are a purposive sample, not county prevalence estimates.
- The [Archives’ B0554 finding aid](https://findingaids.nysed.gov/do/12e4a6bf-5743-5329-ac8c-80978a0cb3ab) documents the 1911 Capitol fire’s destruction of Education Department district-formation records and a later effort to reconstruct them from local records. This supports a history of **documentary loss and reconstruction**, not a claim that the fire caused centralization. Moravia’s B0494 entry explicitly identifies a secondary history as its source where original papers had not been received; it does not say the fire caused that particular absence.
- Administrative centralization and the closing of a particular local classroom are separate questions. Pavilion and Terry Clove offer useful, differently documented examples. At Pavilion, a 1936 newspaper account survives here as a later transcription, not yet as an inspected issue scan. At Terry Clove, a retrospective pupil account and a separate undated news clipping support continued local instruction after the district joined Downsville.
- A separate offline-first Leaflet prototype and a small historical map pilot were built and tested as research tools. Their county outlines and approximate site markers must not be mistaken for surveyed historical school-district boundaries or exact building footprints.

## 1. Scope, access, and method

The work reviewed the public [Common School Archive repository](https://github.com/zmuhls/cs-archive), its [collection index](https://zmuhls.github.io/cs-archive/collections/), the accessible B0494 extract, related publicly accessible archival scans and finding aids, published state plans, local historical accounts, period-map images, and selected contemporary reports as available. A bounded inventory also examined project copies and indexes in connected storage. Those checks did not locate an adjacent B0494 scan; they do not prove no other copy exists. Opaque backups and every potential repository were not exhaustively searched. Existing source files were not edited as part of the research.

All 116 positions in the excerpt received page-level review. The OCR was audited programmatically and assembled as unverified fragments for triage; a targeted subset of fields, not every ledger row, was checked against images. Brackets, ditto marks, overwritten text, crossed-out column headings, repeated exposures, and later annotations require interpretation against the page image. A crossed-out entry is not automatically a legally vacated action, and a bracket containing multiple predecessor districts is not automatically multiple new districts or multiple events. In the selected table below, dates are labeled by role wherever the scan or corroborating source permits. An unknown role stays unknown.

## 2. Fire, reconstruction, and separate record chains

The [B0554 finding aid](https://findingaids.nysed.gov/do/12e4a6bf-5743-5329-ac8c-80978a0cb3ab) connects the loss of district-formation records in the March 29, 1911 State Capitol fire with attempts to reconstruct information from local records, and identifies B0494 as related evidence. The [State Library’s fire collection](https://nysl.nysed.gov/msscfa/sc10867) supplies broader context for the disaster. Reconstruction was selective and uneven; neither the finding aid nor the register proves which local document supplied any particular entry.

A separate annual-report route was enacted **before** the fire. Laws of 1910, chapter 607, section 395(14), directed district superintendents to obtain trustees’ annual reports from town clerks, abstract them for the Education Commissioner, and deposit reports and a copy of the abstract with the county clerk. The office began in 1912. See the [federal compilation, printed pp. 51–54](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I16-bea1ff8b7a397b82355ebd777ba6f9f9/html/GOVPUB-I16-bea1ff8b7a397b82355ebd777ba6f9f9.htm). This statutory route is a lead for surviving reports, not proof of their survival, their use in reconstruction, or a substitute for the separate establishment and alteration files, which may contain petitions, notices, affidavits, orders, and minutes.

The surviving [South Kortright/Stamford local records](https://github.com/zmuhls/cs-archive/blob/main/raw/scans/NYS%20Archives/A4645/South-Kortright-Roll-13.pdf#page=21) include an early district report and 1833 local meeting minutes. These show local reporting and decisions long before the fire. A later B0494 Stamford No. 2 grouping is a geographic and numerical lead, not proof of uninterrupted legal identity between the two records or that those minutes informed the register. The [NYPL catalog entry for MssCol 3200](https://archives.nypl.org/mss/3200) describes one volume of Wolcott 13 and Rose 4 minutes and Rose trustees’ reports, 1823–1839. It is a promising paired-source comparison; item inspection and digitization were not established in this pass.

### Why dates and ballots must remain separate

The [1958 Master Plan’s explanatory history](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=17) states that a 1944 statutory change altered the sequencing of designation and effectiveness. Franklinville’s [register entry](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/zmuhls/cs-archive/main/raw/scans/NYS%20Archives/B0494/District-Consolidation-Data_100-116.pdf#page=9) and [plan](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=77) exemplify designation May 2, 1944 before the June 6 meeting and constituent effective date. This rule is not a license to silently correct a source: the [Little Valley plan](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=80) prints 1946 for the meeting but 1945 for constituent effective dates. Record the conflict until independently resolved.

Votes also need their denominator and authority. In [*Finley v. Spaulding*](https://hallapproved.com/ny/cases/supreme-court/1948/5587291/), 473 voted yes, 470 no, and four ballots were blank among 947 qualified voters present; the chair declared the proposition lost and the trial court granted a temporary injunction. The [appellate decision](https://hallapproved.com/ny/cases/appeals/1948/5545313/) reversed on jurisdiction without deciding whether the proposition passed. The final administrative disposition remains unverified. A numerical lead is not automatically a legal outcome.

## 4. Local schools after centralization

### Pavilion: preferences, a roster discrepancy, and subsequent use

B0494 records the Pavilion grouping’s layout October 14, organizing meeting October 28, and designation November 6, 1935. The later [NYSED Master Plan](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=308) gives a vote of 405–38, with two blank or void. It does not name the October meeting venue. A separate later bond and site vote and temporary classes are associated with Odd Fellows Hall in period reports transcribed in the [district’s history compilation](https://core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2146/PCSD/2756236/PAVILION_DISTRICT_NO.2_-_parts_1_and_2.pdf#page=49); the hall’s parcel and the October meeting venue remain unverified.

The same compilation transcribes the *Wyoming Reporter* of March 25, 1936: Pavilion 1, 7, 10 and 11 preferred closure; Pavilion 5 and 6 favored retaining grades 1–6 for at least another year; Bethany 1 and 8, Covington 3, 5 and 6, York 4, and Middlebury 11 left the fall closing question to the central board. The transcription names 13 districts while its introduction says 14 rural districts. The state plan’s original roster consists of those 13 plus UF 2 Pavilion/Covington, the village district. This comparison provides a plausible explanation for the numerical gap, but does **not** prove the newspaper meant to include UF 2 in its rural count or assign UF 2 a preference. The original issue has not been inspected. [Sources: district compilation](https://core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2146/PCSD/2756236/PAVILION_DISTRICT_NO.2_-_parts_1_and_2.pdf#page=50); [state roster](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=308).

A September 2, 1936 *Le Roy Gazette-News* report, also transcribed in the district compilation, names Pavilion 5 and Middlebury 11 as keeping grades 1–6 open. An October 28 *Perry Herald* report transcribed in a [Middlebury 11 history](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2146/Pcsd/2242ca67-899d-454a-aad9-763a0cb534a2/MIDDLEBURY_DIST_No_11_-_Transit_Rd.pdf?disposition=inline) describes Middlebury 11 operating as a Pavilion Central unit. These support continued instruction after the March delegation, but do not identify the intervening decision-maker or date. Pavilion 6’s omission from the September pair does not by itself prove closure. These source extracts are typeset transcriptions, not original newspaper scans or meeting minutes.

### Downsville and Terry Clove: organization versus local retention

The [ledger](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/zmuhls/cs-archive/main/raw/scans/NYS%20Archives/B0494/District-Consolidation-Data_100-116.pdf#page=44) records Downsville’s July 21, 1938 layout, August 2 meeting and August 3 designation. The [NYSED plan](https://www.ocmboces.org/tfiles/folder1613/1958masterplanPartA_I.pdf#page=199) reports 289–42, zero blank or void, and operation in 1939–40. A separate August 12 bond election authorized $102,300 according to the [*Commercial and Financial Chronicle*, August 27, 1938](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/docs/publications/cfc/cfc_19380827.pdf#page=141); its tally and venue are not stated there. A [Colchester Historical Society account](https://colchesterhsny.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/21-downsville-central-school-1938-wpa-project.pdf) retrospectively reports nine local votes in 1939 to keep first-six-grade instruction; it does not name those nine or provide individual minutes, dates or tallies. The claim remains to be checked against original local records.

Hamden district 7, Terry Clove, is separately listed as an original constituent effective August 3, 1938. In a [2000 recollection](https://www.dcnyhistory.org/schjohnjack.html), former pupil Harriett Tait Schultz describes grades 1–6 at Terry Clove after she began school in 1938. A separate [undated fire clipping](https://www.dcnyhistory.org/images/sch_terry_clove_burns.jpg) reports an earlier narrow non-closure vote and nine pupils temporarily transferred to Downsville after a fire. The handwritten apparent date on the clipping has not been independently verified; neither that earlier vote’s date and venue nor the legal effect of a later remembered Delhi/Downsville choice is established. The two cases illustrate the need to distinguish district organization from classroom operation; their evidence and circumstances differ and do not establish a common policy or motive.

## 5. Geography, prototypes, and uncertainty

The working Leaflet atlas presents source-linked records, dates, filters, and a list alongside a map. Its base polygons use generalized **2024 U.S. Census county boundaries** as modern reference geography, not historical districts. An optional historical viewer uses six dated [Newberry Atlas of Historical County Boundaries](https://publications.newberry.org/ahcbp/) snapshots as **county** boundaries; they are not school-district boundaries and should not be treated as constant over an entire evidence period. Original code and third-party data have separate licenses: the prototype code is MIT-licensed, Leaflet has its own license, and Newberry geometry carries CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 attribution and use conditions. Linking a source does not grant permission to republish its scan.

The version 5 research prototype has 160 indexed records, including 96 grouped ledger sections and 19 site/event leads. Five approximate records share four markers; 14 leads remain unpinned. A marker can be an explicitly labeled road-junction proxy, not an exact building footprint or the site of a meeting. Moravia’s two separate 1868 and 1869 meetings share one approximate Main/Central junction reference, inferred by connecting the venue name with an earlier map; it is neither proof of building continuity nor the location of the school site chosen in 1869. Gregorytown’s junction proxy represents a former schoolhouse area, not the venue of the 1938 Downsville vote or proof the building was open then. Subsequent research identified a Terry Clove fork proxy, likewise not a surveyed footprint; it is not part of the version 5 snapshot. Uncertain places remain unpinned.

A separate pilot traces one **Clarkson District 4** depiction on the [1887 Beers Monroe County map at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2013593279/). It separates the mapped district label and dashed/color edge from the school symbol. Five controls were used for an affine fit; three withheld crossing checks yielded approximately 164 m RMS and 204 m maximum discrepancy. These are checks against selected correspondences, not a confidence interval, legal boundary, cadastral survey, or guarantee of accuracy elsewhere. The pilot is one interpretive map reading, not a statewide layer. The prototype was tested from locally extracted files in Chromium for desktop/mobile and keyboard operation and for no external startup requests; these are functional checks, not an independent historical validation.

## 6. What remains to be done

1. **Locate and inspect the rest of B0494.** Confirm the volume/reel sequence and missing folios through archival catalog and reading-room procedures; record the exact source, not just matching titles.
2. **Trace individual legal files and local records.** Compare selected B0494 entries to B0554 notices, petitions, minutes, orders, and later changes. Match town, district, dates and legal role before asserting continuity or intent. Investigate local trustee and county-clerk holdings separately from formation files.
3. **Resolve the Pavilion count and dispositions.** Inspect the original March 25, 1936 *Wyoming Reporter* and adjacent issues, then central-board and local minutes. The [New York State Library catalog](https://nysl.nysed.gov/nysnp/all/461) lists the relevant newspaper on microfilm and a museum’s paper holdings; catalog coverage is not the same as an examined issue. No issue-by-issue search was made here.
4. **Test the Downsville and Terry Clove decisions.** Locate original 1939 predecessor meeting minutes, contemporary reporting, a dated original of the fire clipping, and records of the later remembered choice. Do not infer that the society’s nine unnamed districts include any specific school.
5. **Find verifiable venue locations.** Compare period directories, deeds, fire-insurance maps, photographs, and meeting notices; keep building, meeting, school site and junction proxy as distinct entities. Do not expose present-day residential addresses merely because they may overlap historic sites.
6. **Investigate actual district boundary evidence.** The [NYSA 11120 finding aid](https://iarchives.nysed.gov/xtf/pdf/11120.pdf#page=3) describes county study files and maps, but does not establish an individual sheet’s date, location, legend, or whether a line was enacted. Verify those details and corresponding orders before publishing district geometry.
7. **Build an auditable data model.** Store each observed date with its role and citation; link actions, predecessor and successor districts, filing and affected counties, source conflicts, ballot counts, stated denominators, procedural outcomes, and uncertainty. Retain literal source values and unknowns. Publish rates only after defining comparable coverage and an independent denominator.

## Citation and reuse note

Links point to the underlying public source or the responsible institution’s public description. Newspaper material in the Pavilion compilation is identified as transcription; later accounts are identified as retrospective. The selected cases and mapping outputs are research aids, not substitutes for examining original records. Historical source rights, map licenses and terms of use should be reviewed separately from the license for any original project code.
